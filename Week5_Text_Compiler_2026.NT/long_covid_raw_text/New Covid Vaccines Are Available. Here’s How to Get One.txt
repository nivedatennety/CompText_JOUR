The shots have been approved for people 65 and older, and for younger people with underlying conditions. Others may be able to get them off label.
Updated Covid vaccines are now available, after the Food and Drug Administration approved them in late August.
Moderna and Pfizer, which make the most commonly used Covid vaccines, confirmed that they had shipped doses of the new shots, which can be found at pharmacies and doctors’ offices. Sanofi, which distributes a non-mRNA option called Nuvaxovid (commonly known as Novavax), said it had begun shipping as well.
However, criteria set by the F.D.A. and a legal dispute stemming from past Trump administration actions could complicate access for some people.
Here is what we know. This article will be updated as more information becomes available.
Who is eligible for the vaccines?
The F.D.A. approved the shots for all people 65 and older, and for younger people who have a condition that increases their risk of severe illness from Covid.
The Moderna shot is approved for ages 6 months and older, the Pfizer shot for ages 5 years and older and the Sanofi shot for ages 12 years and older. (A lower-dose Moderna shot, called mNexspike, is also approved for ages 12 and up.)
About 30 percent to 60 percent of Americans have a qualifying condition, F.D.A. officials . Aside from age, a  can increase the risk of severe illness. They include asthma, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity and pregnancy, as well as cancer, immune deficiency, some mental illnesses and various heart and lung diseases.
People who are not formally eligible may still be able to get vaccinated. Doctors can administer vaccines off label, meaning outside F.D.A. approval criteria. But there may be logistical hurdles: Because most Americans who get Covid shots do so at pharmacies, not all doctors’ offices stock them. And pharmacists in some states may be unable, or may decline, to administer shots off label.
Walgreens — one of the nation’s largest pharmacy chains — said it would allow people to self-attest that they met the criteria, without requiring documentation. CVS, another of the largest chains, is currently requiring prescriptions only in Louisiana.
A spokeswoman for Walmart said it would require prescriptions “in some states” but didn’t specify which ones.
Should I get one?
Major medical organizations  for all adults, including during pregnancy; all children under 2; and children over 2 with high-risk medical conditions. It is also appropriate for healthy children over 2 to get a shot if their parents want them to, the American Academy of Pediatrics said.
These recommendations are broader than the F.D.A. approval, but doctors can administer the vaccines to anyone.
Can I get a vaccine at a pharmacy?
In most states, yes.
But some states’ laws restrict pharmacists from giving vaccines that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has not recommended — and the C.D.C.’s vaccine panel is in legal limbo after a federal court  temporarily invalidated the appointments of members chosen by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
A Walgreens spokeswoman said the company would offer the shots freely to adults, though access for children will be limited in some states.
Amy Thibault, the CVS spokeswoman, said that for now CVS would require prescriptions in Louisiana because of state laws.
The governor’s office in Louisiana did not immediately answer questions about whether it would act to ease access.
Pharmacies’ websites let people check availability in their area. But if you see no or limited availability near you, that may not be permanent. Last year, access expanded significantly over several weeks.
Can my children get vaccinated?
The F.D.A. approval includes children 6 months and older with high-risk conditions. Those without high-risk conditions may be able to get the shots off label.
However, some children won’t be able to get vaccinated at pharmacies. They will need to go to a pediatrician’s office or medical clinic, because state laws vary on the limits of pharmacists’ authority to vaccinate.
Walgreens, for example, will vaccinate children as young as 3 in many places, but in more than a dozen states, it will have age minimums ranging from 5 to 18, a spokeswoman said. (Its online scheduler will show the criteria in your state.)
Age minimums at CVS pharmacies will also vary by state. But the MinuteClinics that CVS runs inside some of its stores will vaccinate people 18 months and older, including in states with more limited access at the pharmacy counter.
Costco and Walmart didn’t respond to requests for state-by-state policy breakdowns.
Will insurance cover the vaccines?
Probably, but we can’t say for sure for everyone.
In the absence of a recommendation from the C.D.C. panel, private insurers don’t have to cover the shots, said Dorit Reiss, a vaccine policy expert at the University of California College of the Law, San Francisco. However, AHIP, a trade organization that includes most major insurers, said its members would pay for them anyway. UnitedHealthcare, which isn’t part of AHIP, said the same. (Double-check with your insurer, because some employer-sponsored plans may differ from their parent companies.)
Medicare still has to cover the shots, and Grace Davis Jamison, a spokeswoman for the Department of Health and Human Services, confirmed that it would.
The area of most uncertainty is the Vaccines for Children program, which about half of American children rely on for free vaccinations. The C.D.C. ships the doses provided through this program, and if there is no C.D.C. recommendation, it’s not clear whether those shipments will happen, Ms. Reiss said.
Ms. Jamison did not answer questions about V.F.C. coverage.
How effective are the vaccines?
The updated vaccines target the XFG variant of Covid, as  this spring. That should be a good match for the virus that is circulating, said Dr. Ian Mellis, an assistant professor of pathology and cell biology at Columbia University.
XFG was the most common variant in North America as of late August, and closely related variants accounted for most other cases, Dr. Mellis said. The shots should significantly increase antibodies that can prevent Covid and reduce symptoms if you do become infected.
When should I get one?
There isn’t a clear consensus. Ask your doctor if you’re not sure about timing, especially if you’re in a higher-risk group. (If you had Covid recently, wait three months.)
Dr. William Schaffner, a professor and infectious disease specialist at Vanderbilt University Medical Center, said October was the ideal time for both Covid and flu shots, to provide the best protection during the respiratory virus season. Protection wanes after a few months.
But Dr. Peter Chin-Hong, a professor and infectious disease specialist at the University of California, San Francisco, recommended that high-risk people get vaccinated against Covid right away, while waiting until October for a flu shot. (It would be reasonable for people under 65 with no high-risk conditions to time their Covid shot more “strategically” ahead of the holidays or travel, he said.) While we can expect a winter wave, he noted, there is also a summer wave happening now.
Are the vaccines safe?
Yes. Like all vaccines, Covid shots can have side effects, almost always mild and brief. But a large body of research shows the benefits outweigh the risks.
It’s common to have a sore arm or flulike symptoms for a day or two, as the vaccine stimulates your immune system to build protection against the virus. Very rarely — in a fraction of a percent of patients — Covid vaccines have been associated with heart inflammation. But you  to develop this from Covid than from vaccination.

“The number of very sick people continues to decrease, but we will still see very sick individuals go to the hospital. We will see people die,” Dr. Chin-Hong said. “It’s hard to have an average message.”
