"Long COVID isn't a problem until we can find no other explanation for why millions of people are unable to work, school performance and children's health decline in unison, and no one can remember what it feels like not to be sick all of the time," she wrote. "The climate crisis requires no changes to our consumption patterns until our major cities burn, at which point the solution is to consume more. Our retroactive responses to these interlocking crises are both more resource-intensive and less effective than a paced approach."
