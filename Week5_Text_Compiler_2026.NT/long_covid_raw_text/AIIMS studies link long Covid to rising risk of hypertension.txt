Ahmedabad: Long Covid - a condition persisting for three months or more after a severe Covid-19 infection - may increase the risk of hypertension   and cardiac issues,   according to two recent   studies presented at the Cardiovascular Research Convergence (CRC) 2025 held at Gujarat Biotechnology   University. The first study, titled 'New Onset of Hypertension   in Long Covid: A Study from a North Indian Tertiary   Care Centre' by lead author   Krishna Baudh from AIIMS   Delhi, examined 306 patients   aged 18€“72 with a history   of Covid infection. The patients were followed for two years after their initial admission. 
The findings revealed that 19.3% (59 patients) developed   new-onset hypertension   during the follow-up period. More than half of these had required hospitalisation   during acute Covid infection, and 49% needed supplemental oxygen. Hospital   complications were more prevalent in the newonset   hypertension group compared to those with preexisting   hypertension. Only one patient developed diabetes   after long Covid. Researchers noted that the new onset of hypertension   did not appear to affect inflammation levels in patients.   A second study, 'Prevalence   of Cardiac Abnormalities   and Long Covid Symptoms   in Individuals with Pre-existing Hypertension: A Study from a North Indian Tertiary Care Centre' by Preeti Singh from the department of anthropology, University of Delhi, focused on cardiac function in patients.   The study analysed data of 325 Covid patients aged 18€“72 from AIIMS Delhi's post-Covid clinic and cardiology   department. Among them, 21 (6.5%) developed   cardiac abnormalities.   Of the 69 patients (21.2%) with pre-existing hypertension,   8 (11.6%) developed   cardiac abnormalities, suggesting higher prevalence   in this group. Additionally,   56 (17%) hypertensive patients   reported long Covid symptoms such as breathlessness,   fatigue, muscle pain,   weakness, and chest pain, the stated stated. The researchers concluded   that hypertensive patients   recovering from long Covid need regular cardiovascular   monitoring. City-based   experts said the findings reflect trends seen in other medical settings, though more   studies are needed to fully understand the long-term effects   of Covid. "While breathing   difficulties and lung damage   are more apparent, patients   are also reporting both short- and long-term complications   of severe infection," a city physician said.
